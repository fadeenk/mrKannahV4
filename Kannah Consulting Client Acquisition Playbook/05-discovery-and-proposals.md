# Discovery calls and proposals

## 30-minute fit-call structure

1. **Open - 2 minutes.** Thank them, confirm the purpose of the conversation, and ask permission to get specific.
2. **Current state - 8 minutes.** Learn how the team ships, where AI is already used, and what slows delivery or creates reliability problems.
3. **Impact and urgency - 8 minutes.** Understand business impact, deadlines, stakeholders, and the cost of doing nothing.
4. **Fit - 7 minutes.** Reflect the problem in their language and explain the smallest relevant engagement path.
5. **Next step - 5 minutes.** Agree on a paid assessment, a direct retainer proposal, or a dated follow-up. Never end with “I will send something sometime.”

## Questions that expose a strong opportunity

- What would your team ship sooner if this bottleneck disappeared?
- Where are AI experiments getting stuck: adoption, quality, architecture, security, testing, or ownership?
- Which part of the codebase or delivery process is most painful to change?
- What does a missed release, reliability incident, or slow delivery cycle cost the business?
- Who owns the decision, budget, and technical access needed to solve this?

## Qualification rule

Advance only when there is a clear business or delivery problem, a credible owner, decision-maker access, a meaningful time frame, and willingness to pay for a defined assessment or delivery scope. Keep all other relationships warm, but do not donate consulting time.

## Proposal rule

Send a proposal within 48 hours of a strong call. Keep it short:

- Problem and desired outcome.
- Scope and delivery cadence.
- Your responsibilities and the client’s responsibilities.
- Investment and payment terms.
- Assumptions and boundaries.
- The next decision and date.

Offer one recommended option. Add a smaller assessment option only when it helps the buyer reduce risk.
