# Outreach templates

## Rule before sending

Every note needs three things: a specific reason you contacted that person, one relevant outcome you can help create, and a low-pressure ask. Do not use mass-mail language.

## Warm reactivation

> Hi [Name] - I hope you have been well. I am focusing Kannah Consulting on helping SaaS teams use AI to speed up delivery while keeping production systems reliable. You have seen how I work, so I wanted to reconnect. Is there anyone in your network leading engineering at a SaaS company who is dealing with AI adoption, delivery pressure, or a difficult codebase? If someone comes to mind, I would appreciate an introduction. Either way, I would enjoy catching up.

## Targeted cold outreach

> Hi [Name] - I noticed [specific product, hiring signal, engineering initiative, or relevant company context]. I work with SaaS engineering teams that want to introduce AI into their delivery workflow or product without creating reliability debt. I have been brought in to own part of a codebase, accelerate delivery, and make systems more stable. I am not sure whether this is on your roadmap, but would a short conversation to compare notes be useful?

## Follow-up after no reply

> Hi [Name] - circling back in case this got buried. If AI delivery or reliability is not a priority right now, no problem. If it is, I am happy to share the practical approach I use to identify quick wins without turning the conversation into a large transformation project.

## Reply after a referral introduction

> Hi [Name] - thanks for the introduction, [Referrer]. I help SaaS teams adopt AI in their delivery process and production systems while improving speed and reliability. I would love to learn what your team is trying to accomplish and see whether an assessment or embedded engagement would be useful. Are you open to a 30-minute fit call next week?

## Message quality checklist

- Use one real observation about the company or person; do not invent pain.
- State one outcome, not a list of technologies.
- Make one simple ask: a 15- or 30-minute conversation, introduction, or conference meetup.
- Do not attach a portfolio or deck in the first message. Offer proof after interest is established.
